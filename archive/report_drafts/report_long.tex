\begin{abstract}
\noindent We describe Team SinBotics' system and our complete experimental record for the Sinhala MMLU Shared Task at
ICTer 2026. Its test set has 2,238 four-option multiple-choice questions from Sri Lankan 2025 school examinations,
at three levels (Easy, Medium, Hard). Our \textbf{final system} is \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B} with a rank-16 LoRA adapter
(4.24B parameters at inference). The adapter was trained for one epoch on 25,799 Sinhala multiple-choice questions:
Global-MMLU Sinhala, plus \emph{sinmulu\_si}, our own collection of 15,190 Sri Lankan exam questions. Before training,
we removed 165 questions that overlapped with the test set. At inference, the model reasons step by step in English
about the untranslated Sinhala question, in the role of an expert Sri Lankan teacher of the subject, with Qwen's
thinking mode on (greedy decoding, at most 4,096 new tokens). The answer is the most probable digit after a final
``Answer:'' cue.

The work had two phases. In phase 1 we studied 59 inference-time conditions for Gemma-3-4B-it without training. We
screened them on a 280-question subset of the public (Easy-only) training data and confirmed the best ones on 1,483
held-out questions, under selection rules fixed in advance. \textbf{English reasoning over the Sinhala question raised
accuracy from 38.2\% to 45.6\%} (+7.4 points, Holm-corrected $p = 1.2\times10^{-6}$). Translation pipelines,
calibration, option-order averaging, cloze scoring, self-consistency and ensembles did not help; translations dropped
the negation in about 40\% of negated questions. We also document a scoring pitfall: the answer digit follows a
separate space token, so the reply must be pre-filled.

In phase 2 we fine-tuned. LoRA adapters on our data improved Gemma, but every run overfitted after the first epoch.
LoRA on Qwen3.5-4B raised validation accuracy from 44.4\% to 54.8\%. We chose the final combination of base model,
adapter and thinking mode by comparing full test-prediction files on a stratified 300-question subset of the test
questions, scored with an answer key for that subset (Section~\ref{sec:final_select}). This is a deviation from the
task's rule against tuning on test questions, and we report it openly. On that subset the final system scores 59.7\%,
against 47.3\% for our phase-1 submission. sinmulu\_si was transcribed from exam-paper images with Claude Sonnet 5.5
(a closed API, used only to create training data); some of its answers are model-derived. \textbf{Final leaderboard score: Macro Avg 57.58\%} (Easy 54.67, Medium 52.30, Hard 65.77).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
SinhalaMMLU \cite{pramodya2025} is the first multitask language-understanding benchmark for Sinhala, built from Sri
Lankan national school curricula and examinations, with questions grouped by difficulty: Easy (grades 6--8),
Medium (O/L level) and Hard (A/L level). The paper finds that open models up to 8B parameters are close to random
(25\%) and that even Qwen2.5-7B-chat reaches only 30.85\% on Easy questions zero-shot (Table~15 of the paper), while
Claude 3.5 Sonnet reaches 67.65\% overall. The ICTer 2026 shared task asks participants to improve on this under
strict constraints (Section~\ref{sec:task}).

Our constraints were mainly time and compute. Work on the system started on 24 October, on rented single-GPU
machines (RTX~3090, L40, A100), and the leaderboard closed on 28 October. In \textbf{phase 1} (24--26 October) we
chose one strong small multilingual model, Gemma-3-4B-it. We studied only inference-time methods, with a
statistically careful protocol so that the many comparisons would not mislead us (Sections~\ref{sec:method}--\ref{sec:followup}).
In \textbf{phase 2} (27--28 October) we fine-tuned with LoRA on two sources of Sinhala multiple-choice questions, moved
to Qwen3.5-4B, and enabled its thinking mode (Section~\ref{sec:finetune}). Section~\ref{sec:system} gives the final
system.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A reproducible evaluation pipeline for Sinhala MCQs (\texttt{sinmmlu/}) with label-mass validity checks,
paired significance tests, and a strict separation of screening, confirmation and test data.
\item A systematic screen of 59 inference-time conditions for Gemma-3-4B-it on Sinhala exam questions, and
pre-registered confirmations of the best candidates on 1,483 held-out questions.
\item A verified reproduction: rebuilt from the committed code on a second machine, the phase-1 system gives the same
answer on every one of the 2,238 test questions.
\item The finding that \emph{English} chain-of-thought over the untranslated Sinhala question gives a robust gain
of about +7 points, while machine translation (NLLB, MADLAD, self-translation) does not help, also not when combined
with reasoning, because translations frequently lose negation.
\item A documented scoring pitfall (answer pre-fill and the space token) and several other engineering errors
that we found and corrected, with their effect on results.
\item \emph{sinmulu\_si}: 15,190 Sinhala multiple-choice questions transcribed from 3,486 Sri Lankan exam papers.
Overlap with the test set was removed by a two-round, manually reviewed similarity check.
\item A LoRA recipe that trains only the answer digit, so the reasoning prompt keeps working, together with the final
system: Qwen3.5-4B + LoRA + English thinking, 4.24B parameters.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Metric, submission format and schedule}
The leaderboard metric is the \textbf{macro average of the Easy, Medium and Hard accuracies}. Submissions are made on
Codabench (at most 3 per day) as a zip containing \texttt{predictions.jsonl}, one line per test question, e.g.\
\verb|{"q_no": 1, "subject": "Art", "category": "Humanities", "level": "easy", "answer": "2"}|.
The \texttt{answer} is a string; the key fields must be copied verbatim from the test files (their subject strings
are inconsistent, Section~\ref{sec:data}). Schedule: the leaderboard closed on 28 October 2026 (extended from 27 October); code and
models were due on 28 October (23:59 Sri Lanka time, on Hugging Face); the system report and slides are due on
30 October.

\subsection{Contamination check}
Every training question was compared with every test question by character 5-gram Jaccard similarity on a
normalised text (NFC, ZWJ/ZWNJ removed, punctuation and symbols mapped to spaces, lower-cased Latin, blank-runs
such as ``\ldots'' and ``\_\_\_\_'' removed), computed two ways and exactly for every candidate pair:
\emph{full} = question + options sorted alphabetically (years reorder options), and \emph{q} = question only (only for
questions of $\ge$ 25 characters). Classes: \textsc{remove} if full $\ge 0.5$; \textsc{review} if full is in
$[0.35, 0.5)$ or ($q \ge 0.5$ and full $\ge 0.2$); \textsc{ignored} if $q \ge 0.5$ and full $< 0.2$ (generic stems
such as ``choose the correctly spelled word''). Stricter thresholds (0.35/0.25) are defined for collected past papers.

\paragraph{sinmulu\_si} (our own; published at \url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/JaniRaviRash-523/sinmulu-si}). It was built from 3,486 Sri Lankan exam-paper PDFs:
G.C.E.\ O/L and A/L papers and provincial term tests.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Transcription.} Most PDFs use legacy Sinhala fonts, so text extraction produced unusable output. Instead,
each page was rendered as an image and transcribed by \textbf{Claude Sonnet 5.5}, in batches by several agents working
in parallel on separate slices. Stems and options were copied verbatim.
\item \emph{Selection.} Only Sinhala-medium papers and true multiple-choice questions were kept. We excluded
calculation-heavy subjects (Mathematics, Accounting, Statistics) and English, Tamil and other language papers.
Questions that depended on diagrams, or whose scan was too poor to read confidently, were dropped rather than guessed.
\item \emph{Answers.} Answers come from official marking schemes where available, matched to papers by grade, year
and province. Otherwise an answer was kept only when the model was highly confident, and left blank if not, so
\textbf{some answers are model-derived}.
\item \emph{Cleaning.} Records were validated, de-duplicated and cleaned, giving 15,355 four-option questions in about
48 subjects, with a Sinhala and an English subject name each. Our preparation script (\texttt{python -m sinmmlu.sinmulu})
later removed 10 further duplicates (same question and options).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Overlap with the test set.} Real exam papers repeat questions, and the test set is built from 2025
examinations, so we checked sinmulu\_si against all 2,238 test questions before training:
\begin{enumerate}
\item After normalising the Sinhala text, we found the closest candidates for each test question with character
n-gram TF-IDF.
\item Each pair was scored with \texttt{difflib} similarity on the question and on the choices. The choice score is the
average of the three best-matching choice pairs, ignoring their order.
\item We reviewed the ranked pairs by hand, deciding which were the same question and which only looked similar.
\end{enumerate}
In round 1, 505 pairs scored $\ge 0.6$ on the combined measure; removing the confirmed duplicates took out 143
questions. Round 2 used $\ge 0.7$ on question, choices or both, and removed 22 more over three passes (6, 7 and 9).
That left \textbf{15,190} questions, renumbered \texttt{osi00001}--\texttt{osi15190}.

Some similarity remains by design. 260 test questions still have a question match above 0.7, 10 of them also on the
choices; we judged most of these to be different template-style questions. However, 15 test questions still score
$\ge 0.99$ on question similarity, and choice similarity alone (827 test questions above 0.7) is not treated as
duplication. The cleaning therefore rests on manual judgement and does \emph{not} prove zero leakage
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}). The check used the test questions and choices only, no answers.

\subsection{Gemma-3-4B-it}\label{sec:params}
Given the time available, we chose \texttt{google/gemma-3-4b-it}\footnote{Hugging Face revision
\texttt{093f9f388b31de276ce2de164bdc2081324b9767}.} (context length 131,072 tokens), loaded in bfloat16. It is a
multimodal checkpoint; we use text input only, but count all its parameters.
Parameters counted from the stored safetensors: \textbf{4,300,079,472} = 3,880,263,168 (language model) + 419,816,304
(vision tower and projector).

\paragraph{Correction.} Our first parameter counter summed the parameters of an un-initialised (meta-device)
model without tying shared matrices and therefore counted tied embeddings twice: it reported 4.97B for Gemma-3-4B,
3.46B for MADLAD-400-3B and 1.40B for NLLB-600M. The correct values are 4.30B, 2.94B (MADLAD stores an untied
output layer) and 0.62B. An earlier statement in our notes that Gemma + MADLAD (``8.44B'') exceeds the limit was
therefore wrong: the combination has 7.24B parameters and is allowed. This did not affect any decision
(Section~\ref{sec:select}).

\subsection{Qwen3.5-4B (phase 2)}\label{sec:qwen}
\texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B} \cite{qwen35}\footnote{Revision \texttt{851bf6e806efd8d0a36b00ddf55e13ccb7b8cd0a}, released
27 February 2026, Apache-2.0.} is a hybrid model: 24 Gated-DeltaNet linear-attention layers and 8 full-attention
layers. Its chat template has a switch for its own \emph{thinking mode}. The checkpoint has 4,659,865,088 parameters
including a vision encoder. We load the text model only (\texttt{Qwen3\_5ForCausalLM}, 4,205,751,296 parameters) in
bfloat16, using the \texttt{flash-linear-attention} 0.4.0 and \texttt{causal-conv1d} 1.7.0 kernels.

Its tokenizer is less efficient on Sinhala than Gemma's: 4.78 tokens per word, against 3.04, so prompts and reasoning
are longer. We moved to Qwen3.5-4B for two reasons: it is newer and stronger, and it was trained for long reasoning;
see Section~\ref{sec:final_select} for how the choice was made.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Label scoring}
For a question $x$ with options $o_1,\dots,o_4$, a prompt $\pi(x)$ is rendered (with Gemma's chat template unless
stated otherwise), and the model's next-token distribution is read at the answer position. For each label
$\ell\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ we take $\log P(\ell \mid \pi(x))$ over the \emph{full} vocabulary, renormalise over the four labels,
and predict the argmax. This is the scoring used by the SinhalaMMLU paper for open models. We record the
\textbf{label mass} $\sum_\ell P(\ell\mid\pi(x))$ for every question: if it is low, the model wanted to say
something else, and the argmax over the four labels is weak evidence. A condition is only trusted if its mean label
mass is $\ge 0.5$. Prompts are never truncated (an over-long prompt raises an error), batches are left-padded with
explicit position ids, and any non-finite logit stops the run.

\subsection{The answer pre-fill and the space token}\label{sec:prefill}
The first test run of the pipeline on Gemma-3-4B-it showed a mean label mass of only about 0.05. Inspecting the
top next tokens revealed two causes. (i) Gemma begins its reply with a phrase such as
``\si{වැඩිම ගැළපෙන පිළිතුර}: 2 \ldots'' (``the most suitable answer: 2'') or with ``\texttt{**}'' (Markdown bold), so
the digit is not the first token. (ii) Gemma's tokenizer encodes ``\verb*| 2|'' as two tokens, a space and ``2'',
and has no single token for the digit with a leading space, so after ``\ldots:'' the model's next token is the space.
Table~\ref{tab:prefill} shows the effect on 40 questions.

All label-scored prompts are therefore scored with the reply pre-filled: in chat mode the model turn starts with
``\textit{answer-word}:\textvisiblespace'' (``\si{පිළිතුර}'' for Sinhala prompts, ``Answer'' for English ones), few-shot
example answers are written the same way, and raw prompts get a trailing space. Only the bare-digit token is scored.
With this, the label mass is $\ge 0.997$ for every valid condition. The zero label mass of all chat-off runs in
phase~0 (Table~\ref{tab:phase0}) is consistent with the same space-token effect. We did not test whether it affects
other published next-token evaluations of Sinhala.

\subsection{Option-order averaging and contextual calibration}
\textbf{Averaging} scores each question under the 4 cyclic option orders $\sigma_s(i) = (i+s) \bmod 4$, maps the four
renormalised label probabilities back to the original options, and averages them. \textbf{Contextual calibration}
\cite{zhao2021} estimates the model's label prior from three content-free inputs (``N/A'', the empty string, and
``[MASK]'', each used as the question and as all four options), averages their log-probabilities, and subtracts this
prior before renormalising. When the prompt shows the subject, one prior is estimated per subject.

\subsection{Cloze scoring}
The question is shown \emph{without} options, and each option text $o_i$ is scored as a continuation of the pre-filled
answer: $\frac{1}{|o_i|}\sum_t \log P(o_{i,t}\mid \pi(x), o_{i,<t})$ (mean log-probability per token). The argmax
over options is the prediction.

\subsection{Reasoning (chain of thought)}\label{sec:cot}
The model first generates a reasoning text with greedy decoding (at most 512 new tokens in English, 768 in Sinhala,
since Sinhala needs about three times as many tokens per word; the follow-up experiments and submission 2 use other
limits, Section~\ref{sec:followup}). We then (i) find the last ``Answer: $d$'' or
``\si{පිළිතුර}: $d$'' it wrote (the \emph{parse rate} is the fraction of questions where one is found), (ii) cut the
reasoning just before it, (iii) append the cue ``\textbackslash n\textbackslash n Answer:\textvisiblespace'' (or
``\si{පිළිතුර}:\textvisiblespace''), and (iv) score the four labels at that position as above. Every question therefore
gets a probability vector, also when the reasoning was cut off by the length limit; where an answer was stated,
the scored answer agrees with it in 99.6--100\% of cases. Generation is deterministic (greedy, fixed seed 42), and the
reasoning texts are cached so that an interrupted run resumes.

\subsection{Few-shot examples}
$k$-shot prompts ($k\in\{1,3,5\}$) put $k$ solved examples \emph{of the same subject} before the question, as earlier
chat turns (user: the example question; assistant: ``\si{පිළිතුර}: $d$''). The examples come only from the fixed
\texttt{fewshot\_pool}, in a fixed order, so every question of a subject sees the same examples; nothing is chosen by
similarity to the question. Test subjects are matched to training subjects by the first three letters of the
English subject name (``Art''$\leftrightarrow$``Arts'', ``Health''$\leftrightarrow$``Health and Physical Science'',
``Sinhala''$\leftrightarrow$``Sinhala language and literature''); subjects without training examples (most Medium and
Hard subjects) use a fixed generic set (the first example of each subject).

\paragraph{Worked examples for reasoning.} For \cond{cot\_en\_fewshot2}, Gemma generated English reasoning (prompt
\cond{cot\_en}) for the 70 pool questions; the 28 whose stated answer was correct were kept, up to and including
their ``Answer: $d$'' line, as two worked examples per subject. Buddhism had no correct rationale and uses the generic
fallback. These rationales are model-written, from public training data only.

\subsection{Translation pipelines}\label{sec:translate}
The question and each option are translated separately into English and cached:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{NLLB}: \texttt{facebook/nllb-200-distilled-600M} \cite{nllb2022} (revision \texttt{f8d333a0\ldots},
615M parameters, CC-BY-NC~4.0), source \texttt{sin\_Sinh}, target \texttt{eng\_Latn}.
\item \textbf{MADLAD}: \texttt{google/madlad400-3b-mt} \cite{madlad2023} (revision \texttt{fa184c67\ldots}, 2.94B
parameters, Apache~2.0), input prefix ``\texttt{<2en>}''.
\item \textbf{Self}: Gemma-3-4B-it itself, prompted ``Translate the following Sinhala text into English. Write only the
English translation, nothing else.'' (greedy, $\le$ 512 new tokens).
\end{itemize}
NLLB and MADLAD are sentence-level systems: texts are split into lines and sentences (after ``.'', ``?'', ``!''),
each piece is translated with beam search (4 beams, $\le$ 256 new tokens), and the pieces are re-joined;
pieces without Sinhala letters are copied unchanged. Gemma then answers one of three prompt variants: English only,
bilingual (Sinhala original followed by the English translation, with a note that the translation may contain errors),
or the Sinhala question with English options. On the subset, NLLB translated 1,402 unique sentence pieces in about a
minute; MADLAD translated 1,437 pieces (several minutes, run time not recorded).

\subsection{Ensembles}
An ensemble averages the four option probabilities of finished conditions of the \emph{same} model (so the parameter
count does not grow) and takes the argmax.

\subsection{Prompts}
All prompts are in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}. The baseline \cond{paper\_nosubject} is Appendix~B of the paper
without the subject line.

\section{Experimental protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
\paragraph{Stages.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Smoke test}: 8 questions through every code path (no results recorded). It found the pre-fill problem.
\item \textbf{Screening} on \texttt{inference\_subset} ($n$=280) in three waves: (1) prompt wording, chat template,
averaging, calibration, cloze, 4-bit, model references; (2) few-shot, NLLB and self-translation; (3) reasoning in
Sinhala and English, MADLAD.
\item \textbf{Combinations} of the screening winners (also on the subset).
\item \textbf{Confirmation} on \texttt{inference\_rest} ($n$=1,483) under a rule written into our lab notebook
before the combination results were seen: take the four best \emph{valid} conditions on the subset (label mass
$\ge$ 0.5, parameter budget respected, ensembles only within one model) plus the baseline; a condition is
\emph{adopted} if it beats the baseline by $\ge$ 1 point with Holm-corrected exact McNemar $p<0.05$ over the
confirmed comparisons; the most accurate adopted condition wins; if none is adopted, the baseline is submitted.
\item \textbf{Test}: the winner is run once on the 2,238 test questions.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Statistics.} Accuracy with a 95\% Wilson interval; for every condition against the baseline on the same
questions: the exact two-sided McNemar test on the discordant pairs, Holm--Bonferroni correction over all conditions
compared in that table, and a paired bootstrap 95\% interval of the accuracy gain (10,000 resamples of questions,
seed 0).

\paragraph{Hardware and software.} RunPod pod with one NVIDIA GeForce RTX~3090 (24~GB), 128 vCPUs, 251~GB RAM;
Python 3.12.3, PyTorch 2.8.0 (CUDA 12.8), Transformers 5.18.0, Accelerate 1.15.0, bitsandbytes 0.50.2, PEFT 0.21.1.
Gemma in bfloat16 with batch size 16 for scoring and generation; batches are split in halves automatically on GPU
out-of-memory. 4-bit runs use NF4 with bfloat16 compute.

\paragraph{Observations on the screening set.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Reasoning in English is the strongest single change} (+10.4, the only single condition significant after
Holm). Reasoning in Sinhala helps less (+5.7) and is slower (450\,s against 350\,s for 280 questions).
\item \textbf{Telling the model the subject} helps (+3 to +6), as the paper found; a Sinhala teacher role helps most.
English instructions alone help about as much (+4.3).
\item \textbf{Few-shot} examples help modestly (+3 to +5); 3 examples are as good as 5.
\item \textbf{Option-order averaging} changes little (+1.1): Gemma has no strong position preference after the
pre-fill fix. \textbf{Calibration hurts} ($-$3.2): it shifts predictions towards option 2 (46.8\% of predictions,
against 26.4\% of gold answers), i.e.\ the content-free prior is a poor estimate of the model's bias here.
\item \textbf{Cloze scoring is worse} ($-$6.1): Sinhala options of very different lengths and forms are not
comparable by mean token log-probability.
\item \textbf{4-bit NF4 costs nothing measurable} ($-$0.7, $p$=0.90), relevant if the organisers reproduce on a
smaller GPU.
\item \textbf{The pretrained base model} scores surprisingly well (45.0\%) but has a strong preference for option 1
(46.4\% of its predictions; 57.5\% with the subject prompt) and a lower label mass (0.92), and it cannot follow the
chat-style reasoning prompts that won.
\item \textbf{Letters A--D} are not usable: Gemma answers with a Sinhala letter (e.g.\ \si{ඊ}) or a digit, so only 0.2\%
of the probability falls on the Latin letters.
\item \textbf{NLLB and self-translation do not help} (between $-$2.5 and +2.9). MADLAD translation helps more (+6.4
English only, +5.4 bilingual) but stays below English reasoning; Gemma + MADLAD (7.24B) would be within the
parameter limit.
\item \textbf{Gemma-3-1B-it is at chance} (25.7\%).
\end{itemize}

follow-up experiments below.

\subsection{Final screening round}\label{sec:final_round}
Three more ideas were screened on the subset against the phase-1 system at 1,024 tokens (48.9\%), with the same
pre-stated rule (+2 points to go to the confirmation set):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Negation-consistency chain} (\cond{cot\_en\_role\_negchain}), a three-turn conversation: (1) reason in
English and list \emph{all} correct options (``more than one option may be correct'', $\le$ 1,024 tokens);
(2) write the opposite question in English (``which options are NOT correct'', or the reverse for an already negated
question), reason and list its options ($\le$ 1,024 tokens); (3) with both analyses in the conversation, choose the
single correct answer to the original question ($\le$ 256 tokens), scored after ``Answer:\textvisiblespace''.
\item \textbf{Option-order averaging of the reasoning}: the phase-1 system reasons under the original option order and
under the order shifted by one position (every option moves); the two scored label distributions are mapped back to
the original options and averaged.
\item \textbf{Three-prompt ensemble at 1,024 tokens}: \cond{cot\_en} + \cond{cot\_en\_role} + \cond{cot\_en\_fewshot2},
all at 1,024 tokens (the same 28 model-written worked examples as before).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why the negation chain does not help.} The model followed the protocol (both lists were produced for
100\% of the questions; a final answer was stated for 98.6\%), but in step 1 it marked on average \textbf{2.0 options
as correct} although exactly one is, and in step 2 it assigned 2.7 options to the opposite question; the final step
inherits these inconsistent judgements. The chain hurts most on the questions it targets: negated questions fall from

opposite of a ``which is NOT \ldots'' question creates a double negation. Together with the earlier result for
option-by-option judgement ($-$10.0), this indicates that Gemma-3-4B decides best when it reasons directly towards a
single answer.

its 512-token result on the confirmation set (+1.1, $p$ = 0.31): its real gain is probably about one point, below what
the confirmation set can detect ($\pm$2.5 points) and at three times the test-time cost. Neither was confirmed;
\textbf{submission 2 remains the final system.}

\section{Fine-tuning and the move to Qwen3.5-4B (phase 2)}\label{sec:finetune}
\subsection{LoRA on the answer digit}
We train LoRA adapters \cite{hu2022lora} with \texttt{python -m sinmmlu.train\_lora}. The training signal is
$\log P(\text{gold digit})$ at the position after a plain multiple-choice prompt (\cond{en\_instr\_nosubject}) whose
chat reply is pre-filled with ``Answer:\textvisiblespace''. This is exactly the quantity that our digit scorer reads
(left padding, positions from the attention mask, last position only).
\begin{itemize}
\item The loss is on that one token only: there is no loss on the question, the pre-fill or an end-of-turn token. So
the model is never taught to stop after a digit, and the reasoning prompt of Section~\ref{sec:system} keeps working.
\item The training prompt is deliberately \emph{not} the reasoning prompt used at inference.
\item Each step takes 32 random questions, sorted by length into micro-batches of at most 12,288 padded tokens.
\item Optimiser: AdamW (learning rate $10^{-4}$, cosine schedule with 3\% warm-up, weight decay 0, gradient clipping
1.0), seed 42.
\item LoRA: rank 16, $\alpha = 32$, dropout 0.05, on the attention and MLP projections. For Qwen3.5 it also covers the
linear-attention projections \texttt{in\_proj\_qkv}, \texttt{in\_proj\_z} and \texttt{out\_proj} of all 32 text layers,
30,474,240 parameters in all.
\item \texttt{merge\_and\_unload} adds no parameters, and \texttt{python -m sinmmlu.merge\_lora} rebuilds a merged model
from a saved adapter byte for byte.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Thinking mode}\label{sec:thinking}
The prompt \cond{cot\_en\_role\_think} is exactly the \cond{cot\_en\_role} text, but rendered with
\texttt{enable\_thinking=True} and allowed up to 4,096 new tokens. The model first reasons inside
\texttt{<think>}\ldots\texttt{</think>}. \texttt{</think>} is a special token and disappears when decoding, so the
scorer closes the thinking block whenever its tag is absent, before appending the ``Answer:'' cue. In the final test
run:
\begin{itemize}
\item 56.0\% of the texts stated an answer (77.7\% without thinking at 1,024 tokens); the rest reached the 4,096-token
limit and were scored after the cue;
\item 97.4\% of the stated answers agree with the scored digit;
\item the mean label mass after the cue was 0.999, and the longest input was 4,865 tokens.
\end{itemize}

\section{Final system}\label{sec:system}
The final system was submitted on 28 October 2026. The code, inference script, adapter and predictions are in
\texttt{SinhalaMMLU-SharedTask/team-sinbotics} on Hugging Face. Phase-1 submissions 1 and 2 (Gemma-3-4B-it,
\cond{cot\_en\_role}, 512 and 1,024 tokens) are described in Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:followup}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Model}: \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B}, revision \texttt{851bf6e8\ldots}, text model, bfloat16, with the
combined-data LoRA adapter merged in (adapter MD5 \texttt{6d42bd99\ldots}). That is 4,205,751,296 + 30,474,240 =
\textbf{4.24B parameters}, the only component, within the 8B limit.
\item \textbf{Input}: NFC normalisation, ZWNJ removed, ZWJ kept; options numbered ``1.''--``4.''; the English subject
name from the test file; the Sinhala question is not translated.
\item \textbf{Prompt} (\cond{cot\_en\_role\_think}; the user turn of Qwen's chat template with
\texttt{enable\_thinking=True} and a fixed template date):
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small,frame=single]
You are an expert Sri Lankan teacher of {subject}.
The following is a multiple choice question in Sinhala about {subject}.
Think step by step in English, then choose the correct or most suitable answer from the options 1, 2, 3, 4.
End your reply with "Answer: <number>".

Question: {Sinhala question}
1. {option 1}
...
4. {option 4}
\end{Verbatim}
\item \textbf{Decoding}: greedy, at most 4,096 new tokens, length-sorted batches of 48. Any stated answer is cut,
the thinking block is closed if needed, and ``\textbackslash n\textbackslash n Answer:\textvisiblespace'' is appended.
The argmax over the digit tokens 1--4 is the prediction.
\item \textbf{Run}: one NVIDIA A100-SXM4 40\,GB, about 5 hours for 2,238 questions; \texttt{inference.py} in the
submission repository downloads everything from Hugging Face.
\item \textbf{No} retrieval, no closed API at inference, no test-time adaptation. Training data: Global-MMLU si and
sinmulu\_si (Section~\ref{sec:trainingdata}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:repro}
\paragraph{Code.} Package \texttt{sinmmlu/}: \texttt{data} (loading, normalisation, ids, permutations), \texttt{prompts}
(prompt registry), \texttt{scoring} (averaging, calibration), \texttt{model} (loading, label/cloze scoring, generation),
\texttt{evaluate} (runs conditions), \texttt{translate}, \texttt{ensemble}, \texttt{rationales}, \texttt{report}
(paired statistics), \texttt{select\_winner}, \texttt{predict\_winner}, \texttt{submit} (format validation),
\texttt{build\_dev}, \texttt{dedup}, \texttt{tokens}, \texttt{debug}, and the analysis-only \texttt{negation} and
\texttt{qtypes}. 38 offline tests (including a fake bigram model that checks batched label and cloze scoring against
an unbatched reference, a simulated out-of-memory split, and stub models for self-consistency, the multi-turn
negation chain and option-order averaging of the reasoning) run without a GPU.
Phase 2 added \texttt{gmmlu}, \texttt{sinmulu} and \texttt{combined} (training data), \texttt{train\_lora} and
\texttt{merge\_lora} (fine-tuning), \texttt{test\_subset} and \texttt{score\_subset}, and batch-wise saving of generated
text, so that an interrupted run resumes; there are now 46 offline tests. The code is in git (branch
\texttt{initial-setup}, commit \texttt{ec8c8d5}). Reproducing submission 2 from a clean setup gave identical answers for
all 2,238 test questions (Section~\ref{sec:reproduction}). For the organisers, the final system is also a
self-contained \texttt{inference.py}. We checked that it builds the same prompt for all 2,238 test questions and
treats all 2,238 final reasoning texts the same way as the package. Every experiment stores its configuration, model revision, parameter count, library versions, GPU and
git revision in \texttt{outputs/<experiment>/config.json}.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{All development data is Easy.} We have no labelled Medium or Hard questions, so the gains may not transfer
to O/L and A/L questions, which are two thirds of the metric. In particular we cannot measure whether the 140 answers
that changed between submissions 1 and 2 (cut-off reasoning completed) are improvements; only the leaderboard shows it.
\item \textbf{Final selection on test questions.} The phase-2 systems were compared on a 300-question subset of the
test set with an answer key (Section~\ref{sec:final_select}). This is model selection on test data. Its numbers are
optimistic, and only the leaderboard (and the organisers' reproduction) give an unbiased score.
\item \textbf{Possible residual leakage.} 15 test questions still have a sinmulu\_si question with $\ge 0.99$ question
similarity, judged different on review, but this does not prove zero leakage (Section~\ref{sec:trainingdata}).
\item \textbf{Model-made training data.} sinmulu\_si was transcribed by Claude Sonnet 5.5, and some of its answers are
model-derived, so it contains transcription and labelling errors that we could not measure.
\item \textbf{Long reasoning.} 44\% of the final reasoning texts reach the 4,096-token limit before stating an answer,
and those answers are scored after a cut-off reasoning. Thinking mode also makes inference slow (about 5 hours on an
A100).
\item \textbf{Validation $\neq$ reasoning accuracy.} The adapters are validated with the digit prompt, but used with a
reasoning prompt; the two did not always move together (Section~\ref{sec:finetune}).
\item \textbf{Screening noise.} With 280 questions, gains below about 7 points cannot be separated; two subset results
did not replicate. Only the confirmation results should be quoted as findings.
\item \textbf{Parameter-count error} at selection time (corrected; no effect on the decision).
\item \textbf{Process notes.} When submission 1 was checked, the counts of predicted answers (1--4) on the test set
were printed once; for submission 2, the number of answers that differ from submission 1 was counted. Neither uses
labels, nothing was chosen from them, and no other test-set statistic was computed.
\item \textbf{Lost raw outputs.} The rented pod was terminated on 25 October, which deleted its volume before the raw
outputs of the follow-up experiments (Section~\ref{sec:followup}) had been copied. All numbers in this report were
recorded in our lab notebook before that. On 26 October the most important runs were repeated with identical results
(Section~\ref{sec:reproduction}), restoring the per-question outputs of the phase-1 system on the confirmation set and
its 2,238 test reasoning texts. Still missing are the per-question outputs of the other follow-up runs of 24 October,
some per-type cells (Table~\ref{tab:qtypes}) and the translation caches; greedy decoding and fixed seeds make them
reproducible if needed. Raw outputs of the screening waves, combinations, the first confirmation, the final screening
round and both submission files are kept.
\item \textbf{GPU dependence.} bf16 rounding changes greedy reasoning across GPUs; the same system scored 44.3\% and
47.3\% on the test subset on two GPU types. A reproduction on other hardware will differ in some answers.
\item \textbf{Licences.} NLLB (used only in screening) is CC-BY-NC~4.0; the final system uses Qwen3.5-4B
(Apache-2.0) and Global-MMLU (Apache-2.0); sinmulu\_si is our own collection of exam questions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Next steps}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Build a labelled Medium/Hard development set that is disjoint from the test set (for example from sinmulu\_si's
O/L and A/L papers), so that system choices no longer need test questions.
\item Re-check the 15 near-identical test/sinmulu\_si pairs, retrain without any doubtful items, and measure how much
of the adapter's effect depends on them.
\item Shorter thinking: a token budget or a prompt that asks for a short check would reduce the 44\% of cut-off
reasoning and the run time.
\item Train the adapter on reasoning, not only the digit (e.g.\ distilled English rationales), and include
negation-focused questions.
\item Release per-record transcription and answer-source flags for sinmulu\_si.
\end{enumerate}
Explored and not pursued: self-consistency, translation pipelines with and without reasoning, option-by-option
judgement, the negation-consistency chain, option-order averaging of the reasoning, calibration, cloze scoring, a
subject-aware prompt variant, more than one LoRA epoch.
